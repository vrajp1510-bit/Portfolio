Assignment 1

Gold, stocks and inflation

Monthly stock returns, gold prices and CPI, January 1970 to August 2025 (668 months).

  • Return and risk. Gold averaged 0.86% a month with a standard deviation of 5.67%. Stocks averaged 0.95% with 4.55%. Gold gave a lower return for more risk.
  • Against inflation. Average annual gold return was 10.1% against 3.6% inflation, yet gold beat inflation in only 30 of 56 years. The 1979 return of +166% lifts the average a lot.
  • Link to inflation. The monthly correlation between gold returns and inflation was 0.12, so gold looked like a loose hedge at best.
  • Real price. Adjusted for CPI, the gold price rose about 13 times, from 0.92 to 11.83.

Assignment 2 · with a partner

Market history and investing rules

Monthly U.S. market and risk-free returns, January 1927 to December 2024 (1,176 months).

Does the tax treatment matter?

I grew $100,000 for 10 years at 8% in two accounts. One pays the 20% tax once at the end. The other pays it every year, so it grows at 6.4%.

AccountValue after 10 years
Tax paid once, at the end$192,714
Tax paid every year$185,959
Difference$6,755

What if you miss the best or worst months?

One dollar invested in 1927 grew to $13,418 by 2024. Removing the 12 best months cut that to $1,338. Removing the 12 worst months raised it to $182,367.

Lump sum or monthly investing?

I simulated 5,000 one-year outcomes using the market's historical mean (0.95% a month) and standard deviation (5.33%), comparing $1,200 invested on day one with $100 a month.

Lump sumMonthly ($100)
Average ending value$1,345$1,278
Worst trial$677$884

The lump sum finished ahead in about 67% of trials. Monthly investing had the better worst case.

Portfolio optimization and hedging

Daily returns for MSFT, IBM, WMT, VZ, SPY, AMZN and QQQ, January 2015 to December 2025 (2,766 trading days).

GoalWeightsDaily returnDaily riskSharpe
Lowest riskIBM 3.6%, WMT 22.5%, VZ 34.3%, SPY 39.5%0.047%0.93%0.051
Highest Sharpe ratioMSFT 33.2%, IBM 5.3%, WMT 38.2%, AMZN 23.3%0.091%1.26%0.072
Single-index modelMSFT 38.1%, WMT 35.6%, AMZN 26.4%0.095%n/a0.074
  • The lowest-risk portfolio had about 17% less daily volatility than SPY alone (0.93% against 1.12%).
  • AMZN had the highest risk of the seven (2.07%) and the optimizer left it out of the lowest-risk mix.
  • The single-index model gave a similar answer to the full covariance model with fewer inputs. Betas ran from 0.42 (VZ) to 1.19 (MSFT).

Hedging a concentrated position

The scenario was $10 million of capital with $7 million fixed in Amazon (30,327 shares at $230.82). I looked for positions in the other six securities that cut the variance of the whole portfolio the most.

  • The best hedge shorted QQQ (about 96% of capital) and held long positions in SPY, IBM and VZ. QQQ and AMZN have a correlation of 0.75.
  • Variance fell from 4.30×10-4 for Amazon alone to 0.86×10-4. Daily risk dropped from 2.07% to 0.93%, a cut of about 55%.
  • Limits. The weights rely on historical correlations that can shift quickly, and the model ignores the cost of borrowing shares to short. I would shrink the QQQ short a little, accept slightly more variance and depend less on large-cap tech moving together.

Assignment 4

Size and value premiums, interest rates, bank risk

Part 1. Do small and value stocks earn more?

Monthly returns for 15 portfolios (5 by size, 5 by book-to-market, 5 by earnings-to-price), January 1965 to December 2021 (684 months).

Avg. monthly returnSt. deviationCAPM alpha
Smallest companies (Size1)1.16%6.4%+0.11%
Largest companies (Size5)0.91%4.3%0.00%
Highest book-to-market (BM5)1.24%5.5%+0.26%
Lowest book-to-market (BM1)0.94%4.8%−0.02%
  • Size and value premiums. Small companies and value stocks earned more and were also riskier. Small companies were about 50% more volatile than large ones.
  • CAPM did not explain it all. If CAPM held perfectly, every alpha would be zero. Small and value portfolios had positive alphas. The largest and the growth portfolios sat at or below zero.
  • The value premium comes and goes. A long-short portfolio (BM5 minus BM1) earned 0.30% a month overall. It lost 0.17% a month in the 1998 to 2000 dot-com boom, then made 1.11% a month from 2001 to 2004.

Part 2. Interest rates and bank risk

Could the market predict rates?

I used the 2021 yield curve to work out the rates it implied for January 2026 and compared them with the actual rates. The market missed: actual rates were roughly 1 to 2 points higher at every maturity.

How much rate risk does BMO carry?

I used BMO's assets and liabilities by maturity band, took each band's midpoint as its duration and gave items with no maturity a duration of zero.

  • Dollar duration of assets: 3.44 million million-years.
  • Dollar duration of liabilities: 1.15 million million-years.
  • Gap: 2.29 million million-years. A 100 basis point rise in rates would cost shareholders about $22.9 billion.
  • BMO's reported impact was $1.483 billion. My figure is far larger because the model is simple: band midpoints and no hedges.
