271929-03-2819293-0.00550.00340.99451.0034Q 3It does not fully make sense to directly compare earnings yields (E/P) to bond yields because, From the pricing equation, this tells us that the earnings yield depends on two key factors: the required return on equity (r), which includes the risk-free rate plus an equity risk premium, and the expected growth rate of earnings (g). In contrast, the yield on a 10-year Treasury bond reflects only the risk-free rate and does not include any growth component. Since equities are risky and offer growth potential, while Treasuries are essentially risk-free with fixed payments, comparing E/P directly to bond yields ignores important differences such as expected earnings growth, the equity risk premium, and overall risk. Only in the unrealistic case where growth is zero and risk premia are ignored, would the earnings yield roughly match a bond yield. Therefore, although the comparison might provide a rough sense of market conditions, it is theoretically incomplete and potentially misleading.
